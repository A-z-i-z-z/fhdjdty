# Smoothness before and after Wave 0

Written 2026-11-25. This replaces the empty layout that was committed with Wave 0. The numbers are from
the owner's phone (Galaxy S24 Ultra, Android 17, 120 Hz panel, so a frame that is on time takes 8.3 ms or
less), taken with `tools/phone-perf.ps1`. The raw files are on the owner's PC under `dist/perf/`
(git-ignored): `build32/` is before, `w0-owner-debug/` and `w0-owner-debug-run2/` are after. Every figure
below was copied from those `summary.json` files or from the "Numbers" part of `docs/waves/w0/phone-proof.md`;
nothing was estimated. No serial number, account or chat text is in this file (the repository is public).

**Short version:** in these runs Wave 0 did not make the app smoother, and Home got somewhat slower. The phone
was also warmer in the after runs, so this does not prove that the wave made anything worse. Cold start was
faster. Both builds are unoptimised debug builds, so none of this says how the app feels in the optimised
build, which is the one the budgets are written for.

## What was measured, and how

- **Frames.** `phone-perf.ps1` resets and reads `dumpsys gfxinfo <package> framestats` every 400 ms while a
  scenario runs and merges the frames by their vsync time (the phone keeps only the last 120). A frame is
  "within" when it took 8.3 ms or less. Each scenario ran 3 times and the three runs are added together.
- **P1, Home scroll:** swipe the Home screen up and down (the living scene, the cards, the recent chats). Its
  budget: at least 95 % of frames within 8.3 ms and no frame over 33 ms. The budgets apply to the optimised
  build.
- **P7, Home idle:** Home on screen, untouched, for about 10 s per run. This is the cost of the animated scene
  alone (stars, fire, embers, fireflies). No budget.
- **Cold start (P6):** Hermes was stopped, the app sent to the background and killed (`am kill`), then
  `am start -W` read the launch time. One sample each, taken by hand the same way both times. Budget in the
  plan: under 700 ms.
- **Streaming a long answer (P4):** measured after the wave only, on the chat screen while the practice model
  streamed a 14 s answer.
- Not run: P5 (opening a chat, going back, switching tabs), because it opens one of the owner's chats and the
  before run had no P5 to compare with; the tool's own cold-start scenario, because it force-stops the app,
  which would cancel the owner's alarms; P2 and P3 (they need the test twin's seed data); memory (not measured).

## What the numbers do and do not show

They show:

- Home idle is slower per frame after the wave, in both repeats (11.0 and 11.1 ms against 9.7 ms).
- Home scroll has a worse tail after the wave (worst frame, frames over 33 ms), and a middle that is 0.6 to
  0.9 ms higher.
- Cold start was faster, once.
- The Home scroll budget is missed before and after. The app has never met it in a debug build.

They do not show:

- That the wave caused the slowdown. The after phone was about 9 degrees warmer, the battery state differed,
  and the two after runs disagree with each other on the tail. A regression of the scene is neither proven nor
  excluded.
- Anything about the optimised build the owner will run. Both builds are unoptimised debug builds
  (`isMinifyEnabled = false`, debuggable), and neither had a compile filter applied.
- Anything about opening a chat, going back, switching tabs, or the bottom sheets (P5 was not run; and the
  sheets did not become visible at normal animation speed in the proof, a defect that is being fixed).
- Anything about streaming before the wave.

## Why the scene could cost more now (hypotheses, not findings)

How a frame of the scene is produced (`app/src/main/java/dev/pocketagent/app/ui/pixel/`): the Home scene is a
small grid of "stage pixels" (an `IntArray` in `stage/PixelStage.kt`), each one 3 to 4 device pixels wide. A
clock (`SceneClock.kt`) lets at most 30 ticks a second through. At each tick the draw callback in
`HearthScene.kt` asks `StageSurface.draw` (`StageSurface.kt`) for a picture: if the scene state or the time
changed, it clears the stage, `SceneRenderer.render` (`stage/SceneRenderer.kt`) paints sky, stars, moon, ridges,
fire, glow, mascot and particles into the array on the main thread, the array is copied into the next of three
`Bitmap`s with `setPixels`, and that bitmap is drawn scaled up without smoothing. The up-scaling is done by the
graphics hardware (the CPU routine `scaleInto` is only used for the widget bitmap).

The most likely costs, in the order I would test them:

1. **The whole stage is repainted on the main thread at every tick.** `SceneRenderer.kt:73` copies a sky
   gradient over the full grid, then every star gets a `sin` call and a plot (`SceneRenderer.kt:74-86`), then
   the ridges, glow (`:135`) and sprites are blended pixel by pixel. At 30 ticks a second this is steady work on
   the thread that also scrolls Home and handles touch; in a debug build that code runs slower than it will in
   the optimised one.
2. **A new bitmap is uploaded to the graphics hardware at every tick.** `StageSurface.kt:56-66` fills the next
   bitmap of the ring with `setPixels` and draws it; Android has to copy it to a texture each time (the ring
   never reuses a bitmap the hardware may still be reading). The Home scene fills the whole screen width, so the
   copy is not small.
3. **The clock asks for a frame on every vsync.** `SceneClock.kt:44` waits for each frame (`withFrameNanos`) and
   the gate (`stage/FrameGate.kt`) lets only every fourth one pass, so on this 120 Hz panel the screen is woken
   120 times a second to do work 30 times a second. That could explain a higher idle median and fewer frames in
   P7. (`HearthScene.kt:101-104` also builds a new `SceneState` and a `Triple` stamp on every draw: small, but
   constant.)

None of these has been profiled. A trace of Home idle on the twin (main thread share, texture uploads) would
confirm or reject them before anyone changes the scene or adds to it in Wave 1.
